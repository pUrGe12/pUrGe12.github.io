Books

10 shelves · 38 listed · 8 written about

I have written about some of these in more depth — the ones with enough in them to write about. I try to extract meaning from every book I read and it obviously doesn’t work out that well, so I figured a better way is to sometimes just appreciate them too.

What follows is roughly how I’d shelve them: the series I grew up on, the science, the fiction, the self-help I mostly tolerated, and the biographies. The tiles are the lists. The cards sitting next to them are the ones that got a page of their own.

The series I grew up on

5 books

Where it started. I was twelve and these were the whole world.

  • The Percy Jackson universe Rick Riordan

    Every single book. Percy Jackson (5), Heroes of Olympus (5), Kane Chronicles (3), Trials of Apollo (5), Magnus Chase (3), Greek Heroes and Gods (2), and a few of the latest releases which I did not like as much (might just be that I am older now).

  • Geronimo Stilton Elisabetta Dami

    Something like 20 of them. I do not remember the names any more, but I can still recall opening my cupboard and finding all of them looking back at me.

  • Assassin's Creed Oliver Bowden

    Renaissance, Brotherhood, The Secret Crusade (personal favourite, Altair's story) and Revelations, which is where Ezio dies. Read the books before I played the games.

  • Diary of a Wimpy Kid Jeff Kinney

    The whole series, five times over. I was so obsessed I would read this instead of YouTube while eating.

  • Tom Gates Liz Pichon

    Bought Excellent Excuses because I thought it would hand me a LIST OF EXCUSES to make. Turned out to be a good buy either way.

Physics and the universe

4 books

Science books were a good passtime.

  • A Brief History of Time Stephen Hawking

    Possibly the first science book I bought, and I understood jackshit back then.

  • Parallel Worlds Michio Kaku

    Some mind-twisting stuff in there, but I was slowly getting a hang of it.

  • Brief Answers to the Big Questions Stephen Hawking

    Still a doable read by then, on account of my experience.

  • Quantum Manjit Kumar

    Introduced me to quantum mechanics like no other book could. I felt like I was living with Bohr.

Absurdism and the uncomfortable ones

5 books

  • The Trial Franz Kafka

    Haunting. Led me into a deeper study of absurdism and kafkaesque writing. Any screenplay I have thought of since has had this element in it.

  • The Metamorphosis Franz Kafka

    Another one of Kafka's you-cannot-control-it-you-will-die novels.

  • The Plague Albert Camus

    A thoughtful tale, though I did not know how to feel after reading it. It leaves you blank, but you are still glad the town is safe.

  • The Stranger Albert Camus

    One of the weirdest books I have read, and I have never hated a protagonist this much. Even from an atheist's perspective I do not sympathise with his philosophy.

  • Animal Farm George Orwell

    I knew what to expect. It's hard to escape the logic he presented, and that is roughly where we are at this point.

Lives, ordinary and otherwise

5 books

  • The Alchemist Paulo Coelho

    Avoided this one for years for absolutely no reason. Glad I came through, because it genuinely is one of the most moving books out there.

  • A House for Mr Biswas V. S. Naipaul

    Never thought I would enjoy the melodrama, but here we are, because the setting and the plot are so simple and yet so elegant.

  • The Kite Runner Khaled Hosseini

    More raw and emotional than most other stories. A simple plot expressed nicely, though nothing too over the top.

  • The Count of Monte Cristo Alexandre Dumas

    Hope and wait. A story where no character is morally good or evil, only selfish.

  • Dhalgren Samuel R. Delany unfinished

    The one novel I still can't finish. That's all you need to know about this.

Self-help and money

4 books

I didn't really enjoy these much. I read them because my dad had a bunch of them in the house.

  • Think Again Adam Grant

    About the need to rethink old ideas and old ways of thinking. I always took it as a message to break conformity and think for yourself.

  • Atomic Habits James Clear

    Who hasn't read it. Reminiscent of that Admiral McRaven speech at the University of Texas. Some ideas were nice, like the ones for removing distractions.

  • The Psychology of Money Morgan Housel

    Not really self-help I guess, but I do not remember a thing from it. Besides, all of it seems like a part of The Intelligent Investor.

  • The Intelligent Investor Benjamin Graham

    How could I not. The OG self-help book. Reading this, I started my investing journey, with the help of my dad of course.

Biographies, in one line

2 books

Not all of them need a whole page. Some I can say in a line.

  • Masters of Doom David Kushner

    Gave some excellent perspective on how smart Carmack really was, and why the two Johns were the equivalent of the two Steves.

  • That Will Never Work Marc Randolph

    A book about ideas, and about accepting when you are not the right person for one, then finding the right thing to do instead. Honestly a light read.
